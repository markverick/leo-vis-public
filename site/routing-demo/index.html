What this example assumes—and what it demonstrates

ECMP suppression policy: this model poisons or omits toward every active next hop. It does not advertise a separate alternative route per neighbor. That choice is important to the first scene’s result; it is not a claim that RFC 2453 specifies this multipath extension.

The second scene has no admitted gateway contact after the withdrawals, so it compares how failure ends, not whether a policy can deliver. Radio links stay up throughout the shown guard interval; actual radio loss and a complete handover are not simulated here.

Both scenes are illustrative counterexamples, not measured Starlink paths or large-network performance estimates. Disabling poisoned reverse is shown here as no reverse suppression; simple split horizon is a separate column. Gateways originate one shared anycast prefix. A physical contact remains up during the 5-second withdrawal guard; orange contacts are unavailable to routing.

The viewer replays processed events generated offline by the discrete-event routing simulator. Its observation hooks were checked against an unobserved run, and packet paths were checked against native per-hop replay. The browser does not invent or recompute routes.
